# Power of Attorney, Notarization & Legalization — Notes for Foreign Applicants

This explains the formalities that most often confuse foreign clients and overseas correspondent firms.

## 1. Power of Attorney (POA / 商标代理委托书)

- A foreign principal with no residence/business premises in China **must authorize a CNIPA-recorded trademark agent** to act.
- The POA must state the **principal's nationality** and the **scope of authorization** (filing, responding to office actions, opposition, renewal, etc.).
- It must carry the applicant's **signature (individual) or company seal/authorized signature (entity)**.
- The applicant **name and address on the POA must match** the application and the qualification document exactly (in both original language and Chinese translation).
- Define scope clearly up front — a POA limited to "filing" may not cover refusal review or opposition later.

## 2. Foreign qualification document: notarization / legalization / apostille

Whether the foreign company extract must be authenticated **depends on the situation and reciprocity**; practice can vary, so confirm case by case. The typical routes:

1. **Notarization + consular legalization**: document notarized locally → authenticated by the foreign authority → legalized by the Chinese embassy/consulate.
2. **Apostille**: for countries party to the Hague Apostille Convention, an apostille may replace consular legalization.
3. **Reciprocity exemption**: in some situations a clean copy plus a certified Chinese translation is accepted without notarization.
4. **HK / Macao / Taiwan**: local notarization + the designated mainland transmission/confirmation channel.

> Even where notarization is not required, a **properly sealed Chinese translation** is effectively always required, and name/address alignment is non-negotiable.

## 3. Chinese translation requirements

- Translate by a competent translator/translation body; use a **translation seal** where expected.
- Translate the **company name and address into one fixed Chinese form** and reuse it on the application, POA and all attachments.
- Goods/services are translated **into CNIPA standard items**, not literal free translation.
- Keep an aligned bilingual version on file for future filings, renewals and disputes.

## 4. Signature notes

- The application may be signed in Chinese or a foreign language, but the signature must correspond to the authorized person/entity.
- Entity applicants: use the company seal and/or an authorized signatory; record the signatory's title if asked.

## 5. Common defect patterns (avoid these)

| Defect | Result | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese name differs between documents | Correction notice / delay | One fixed transliteration everywhere |
| Foreign-style goods descriptions | Rejected/required amendment | Use CNIPA standard items |
| POA missing nationality or too narrow | Cannot act on that procedure | Re-issue a complete POA |
| No Chinese translation / no seal | Formality challenge | Certified sealed translation |
| Expired/incorrect legalization route | Re-authenticate | Choose apostille vs consular per country |

## 6. What to ask the foreign client / foreign associate up front

1. Country of incorporation and whether it is Hague-Apostille party.
2. Who signs and in what capacity.
3. A fixed Chinese company name and address.
4. Scans of the registration certificate with enough time for authentication.
5. Whether they will also file the Chinese-character mark and in which classes.

*General information only, not legal advice; authentication requirements depend on current CNIPA practice and bilateral arrangements.*
