# Required Documents Checklist — Foreign Applicant Filing a China Trademark

Use this to collect everything before the agent files. Missing/mismatched documents are the #1 cause of correction notices and delays.

## A. Applicant identity

- [ ] **Foreign company**: business registration certificate / certificate of incorporation / extract from the company register (clear scan)
- [ ] **Individual foreign applicant**: passport copy (or national ID)
- [ ] Applicant's full legal **name in original language** AND its **standard Chinese translation**
- [ ] Applicant's full **address in original language** AND Chinese translation
- [ ] **Nationality** of the applicant (required; also stated in the Power of Attorney)

> Critical: the Chinese and foreign **name and address must be identical/consistent across every document**, character for character. Pick one standard transliteration and reuse it everywhere.

## B. The mark

- [ ] Mark specimen (high-resolution JPG/PNG)
- [ ] Black-and-white (broad protection) **or** specified colors (protection limited to those colors)
- [ ] For combined word+device marks: decide whether to also file the word mark separately (recommended)
- [ ] Decide on a **Chinese-language version** of the mark (transliteration/translation) and file it
- [ ] List of classes and the **standard Chinese goods/services items** per class (first 10 included)

## C. Authorization

- [ ] **Power of Attorney (POA)** to the CNIPA-recorded Chinese agent, stating the applicant's nationality; signed/stamped
- [ ] Designated Chinese recipient for service where applicable

## D. Authentication of foreign documents (situation-dependent)

- [ ] Confirm whether the foreign business certificate needs **notarization + consular legalization**, or qualifies for **apostille**, or is accepted under reciprocity without notarization
- [ ] Hong Kong / Macao / Taiwan documents: local notarization + the required mainland transmission procedure
- [ ] **Certified/official Chinese translation** of the foreign document, with translator's seal; names/addresses aligned

## E. Optional / situation-specific

- [ ] Priority document if claiming **Paris Convention priority** (within 6 months of the first filing) + Chinese translation
- [ ] Coexistence/consent letter (if overcoming a cited mark)
- [ ] Evidence of use / fame (helpful for distinctiveness or disputes, not for a clean filing)
- [ ] Madrid: base application/registration number and designation details

## F. Before submission — agent QC (the 3 biggest rejection triggers)

- [ ] **Name/address consistency** across application, certificate, POA, translation
- [ ] **Goods/services items are CNIPA-standard** (not foreign wording) and match the class
- [ ] **Clearance search done** for identical/similar marks (Latin + Chinese + pinyin) in same/related classes

## G. After filing — send the client

- [ ] Acceptance notice + application number
- [ ] Written timeline (≈6–9 months) and realistic expectations
- [ ] Monitoring plan for the publication/opposition window
- [ ] Reminders: keep China-use evidence; renewal at year 10

*General information only, not legal advice; document formalities follow current CNIPA practice and reciprocity arrangements and may vary by applicant country.*
