# Guide 02 — How to Register a Trademark in China (step by step)

## 1. Are you allowed to file it yourself?

A foreign applicant **without a habitual residence or business premises in China must be represented by a trademark agency recorded with CNIPA** (a "Chinese trademark agent"). A foreign company cannot self-file directly from overseas. This is why overseas IP firms instruct a China correspondent.

## 2. Choose your route

| | A. National (direct CNIPA) filing | B. Madrid Protocol designating China |
|---|---|---|
| How | Your Chinese agent files directly with CNIPA | File via your home office → WIPO → China national phase |
| Best when | China is a priority / you want maximum control, or you only need China | You already file many countries via Madrid |
| Coverage | One mark across chosen China classes | Extends an existing base application/registration |
| If refused | Chinese agent handles review in Chinese | A **provisional refusal still needs a Chinese agent** to respond in the China phase |
| Speed/control | Fastest feedback, most flexible goods list | Convenient multi-country bundle |

## 3. The nine-step national workflow

1. **Define the mark and classes** under the Chinese version of the Nice Classification (45 classes; use CNIPA standard goods/services items).
2. **Pre-filing clearance search** — exact + similar marks, Latin + Chinese characters + pinyin, across identical and related classes; produce a risk report.
3. **Sign the engagement / Power of Attorney** (for a foreign principal it must state nationality).
4. **Collect & verify applicant documents** — foreign business registration certificate (or passport), with a proper **Chinese translation**; notarization/legalization may be required depending on the situation (see templates).
5. **File online & pay the official fee** — CNY 270/class for the first 10 items.
6. **Acceptance (受理通知书)** — usually within ~1 month; you get an application number (this is **not** registration).
7. **Formality + substantive examination** — distinctiveness, absolute grounds, conflicts with prior rights.
8. **Preliminary approval & 3-month publication** — third parties may oppose during this window.
9. **Registration certificate** — if no successful opposition; protection lasts 10 years from registration, renewable.

A smooth case typically takes **about 6–9 months** end to end.

## 4. Documents checklist (foreign applicant)

- Trademark application (bilingual applicant name/address)
- Clear mark specimen (JPG; specify color claim or black-and-white)
- Applicant's qualification document (foreign company extract / passport)
- **Chinese translation** of the qualification document
- Power of Attorney to the Chinese agent (nationality stated)
- Priority document if claiming Paris Convention priority (within 6 months)
- Designated Chinese recipient for service where applicable

> The applicant's Chinese and foreign **name and address must match character-for-character** across documents — mismatches are a top cause of correction notices.

## 5. Classification tips (where foreign filers go wrong)

- Use **CNIPA standard items**; USPTO/EUIPO-style descriptions are frequently challenged.
- First 10 items per class are included; each extra item adds an official fee.
- File **core class + Class 35 + realistic related classes**; don't over-file dozens of classes you will never use (3-year non-use cancellation risk — see Guide 03).
- Decide on the **Chinese version of the mark** and file it too.

## 6. Most common rejection reasons

1. Identical/similar prior mark in the same/similar class (clearance should catch this).
2. Non-distinctive / descriptive / generic wording.
3. Goods/services items not standard or unclear.
4. Forbidden signs (national symbols, misleading, adverse social influence).
5. Bilingual name/address mismatch or document formalities.

## 7. After registration: maintenance

- Use it (and keep evidence) — a registration vulnerable to **non-use cancellation after 3 years** of no use.
- Record changes of name/address, assignments and licenses.
- Renew before the 10-year expiry (12-month window + 6-month grace).
- Keep monitoring and enforce on Chinese e-commerce platforms.
