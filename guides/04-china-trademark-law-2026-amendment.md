# The 2026 Amendment to China's Trademark Law — What Foreign Applicants Must Know

> **Status:** The revised Trademark Law of the People's Republic of China was adopted on **26 June 2026** by the 23rd session of the Standing Committee of the 14th National People's Congress (Presidential Order No. 77) and **takes effect on 1 January 2027**. It is the first full revision since the Law originally took effect in 1983, expanding it from 8 chapters / 73 articles to **9 chapters / 87 articles**. Marks already registered before the effective date remain valid (transitional article).
>
> Until 31 December 2026 the **current** Law still applies — plan for both regimes during the transition. This is a planning summary, not legal advice.

## Why it matters to foreign brand owners

For years China's biggest IP pain point for overseas brands has been **first-to-file squatting and defensive hoarding**. The 2026 amendment is explicitly aimed at "heavy on registration, light on use" (重注册、轻使用). It makes life harder for squatters — but it also raises the bar for **legitimate holders who file defensively and then never use the mark**.

## The five changes that affect your China strategy

### 1. Opposition window shortens from 3 months to 2 months
Under the current Law, a preliminarily-approved mark is published for a **3-month** opposition period. From 1 January 2027 this becomes **2 months**. After the window closes the mark registers and your only route is invalidation, which is slower and costlier.

**Action:** speed up trademark watching. A monthly check is no longer enough in 2027 — decisions to oppose must be made within weeks of publication.

### 2. New 5-year use declaration (on top of the existing 3-year cancellation)
The Law retains the existing rule that a mark unused for **3 consecutive years** can be cancelled by any third party (撤三), and **adds an affirmative duty**: around the fifth year after registration the rights holder must **submit evidence of use**. A registration can no longer be treated as a "file and forget" asset.

**Action:** build a dated China-use evidence file now — Chinese-language sales pages, Tmall/JD/Douyin listings, invoices, advertising, packaging photos, customs records.

### 3. No-use-intent filings can be refused; bad-faith filing is fined
Applications that are **not for the purpose of use and clearly exceed normal production/business needs** fall within the scope of refusal. Bad-faith applications that cause adverse impact may draw a **warning and a fine of up to CNY 100,000** (Article 54).

**What this means for defensive strategy:** blanket portfolio registration across dozens of unrelated classes "just in case" is now riskier and may be rejected or attacked. File the classes you genuinely use or have a concrete plan for, and be ready to state the intent. Genuine brand-protection filings for marks you operate are not the target — speculative hoarding is.

### 4. Stronger well-known (驰名) mark protection
Protection against squatting on dissimilar goods is extended so that it applies **whether or not the well-known mark is registered in China**, tightening the route for globally famous brands that were themselves slow to file.

### 5. Non-traditional marks clarified, and abusive IP litigation penalised
- Colour-combination, sound and motion marks that result solely from the nature of the goods, are necessary to obtain a technical effect, or give substantial value to the goods **cannot be registered**.
- Article 81 penalises bad-faith trademark lawsuits (e.g. fabricated claims), including civil liability for losses — aimed at "trademark troll" / 心机商标 enforcement.

## Transition checklist for foreign applicants (2026 → 2027)

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Now – Dec 2026 | Current Law applies: **3-month** opposition window. Finish any urgent filings/oppositions under the familiar rules. |
| Before filing | Tighten class lists to classes with real use/intent; keep a short rationale per class. |
| From 1 Jan 2027 | Use the **2-month** opposition window; expect faster watching decisions. |
| Within ~5 years of each registration | Prepare and file the **use declaration** with evidence; do not let marks sit idle. |
| Ongoing | Maintain a continuous Chinese-use evidence file for every registration. |
