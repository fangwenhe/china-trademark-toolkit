# Guide 03 — Responding to Refusals, Office Actions, Oppositions and Disputes in China

This guide maps the CNIPA decisions a foreign applicant may meet after filing, the **strict deadlines**, and what a Chinese agent can do. Deadlines are short and usually cannot be undone if missed.

## 1. Decision types and deadlines

| Event | What it means | Deadline to act | Typical review time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Correction / formality notice (补正)** | Fix documents/items | Short fixed period stated in the notice | — |
| **Refusal / partial refusal (驳回)** | CNIPA rejects all/part of the application | Apply for **review of adjudication (驳回复审) within 15 days** | ~9 months (+3 extension possible) |
| **Preliminary publication** | Passed examination; open to challenge | Opposable for **3 months** | — |
| **Opposition (异议)** | A third party challenges during publication | Respond within the stated period | ~12 months (+6 possible) |
| **Invalidation (无效宣告)** | Attack an already-registered mark | No fixed short bar; evidence-based | ~9–12 months |
| **Non-use cancellation (撤三)** | Cancel a mark not used for **3 consecutive years** | Registrant must prove use | ~9 months |
| **Madrid provisional refusal** | China-phase refusal of an international registration | Respond in Chinese via a Chinese agent by the stated deadline | As above |

## 2. Refusal review (驳回复审) — when to fight vs re-file

**Fight (request review) when:**
- You have strong distinctiveness or fame evidence the examiner did not see.
- A cited prior mark can be overcome (consent/coexistence letter, the cited mark is being cancelled/withdrawn/expired, or goods are genuinely dissimilar).
- The mark is in use in China and re-filing would lose your priority date.

**Re-file instead when:**
- The blocker is a solid, identical prior mark in identical goods with no way around it.
- You can amend the mark/specification and a fresh filing is faster and cheaper than review.

These are often combined: review the current application **and** file an amended safety application in parallel.

## 3. Evidence that actually helps

- **Use evidence in China**: dated invoices, contracts, customs records, e-commerce listings and sales, advertising spend, packaging.
- **Fame / reputation**: global and China sales figures, media, awards, search volume — translated and notarized where possible.
- **Distinctiveness**: acquired distinctiveness through long, substantial use.
- **Coexistence/consent** from the cited mark owner (persuasive but not binding on CNIPA).
- Proof the cited mark is **bad-faith**, expired, cancelled, or not used (supporting a parallel cancellation).

## 4. Opposition and invalidation (offense and defense)

- **Oppose** a squatter's mark during its 3-month publication window — cheaper and faster than invalidating after registration.
- **Invalidate** an already-registered mark on absolute grounds, prior rights, or **bad-faith filing (恶意注册)** — China has strengthened anti-squatting provisions, and evidence that the filer copies many foreign brands is powerful.
- **Defend** your own registration by filing use evidence and counter-arguments within deadlines.

## 5. Non-use cancellation ("撤三") — use it or lose it

Any party can request cancellation of a mark that has **not been used for 3 consecutive years** without justified reason. The registrant bears the burden of proving real use. Lessons:
- Keep organized China-use evidence for every registration.
- Don't hoard defensive classes you will never use without a plan; prioritize classes you can defend.
- You can also weaponize this to clear a squatter's dormant blocking mark.

## 6. Platform enforcement (where most foreign brands feel the pain)

A Chinese registration (or pending filing) is the key to IP complaints on **Tmall/Taobao, JD, Douyin, Pinduoduo, Xiaohongshu, 1688** and to customs recordal. A Chinese agent can prepare:
- Infringement take-down packages and brand recordals
- Administrative complaint to the local Market Supervision Administration (AMR)
- Customs IP recordal to stop export of counterfeits
- Civil/administrative enforcement referrals

## 7. Decision checklist when a notice arrives

1. Note the **exact deadline** immediately (15 days for refusal review is easy to miss).
2. Get a certified translation of the decision and cited marks.
3. Classify the ground: prior-right conflict / distinctiveness / formality / bad faith.
4. Pull evidence and check the cited mark's status (alive? used? cancellable?).
5. Choose review, amendment + re-file, negotiation/coexistence, or withdrawal — often a combination.
6. Respond in Chinese through your CNIPA-recorded agent; never let a deadline lapse.

*General information only, not legal advice. Timelines follow current CNIPA/trademark-review practice and may change.*
