# Guide 05 — Navigating the 2026 → 2027 Trademark Law transition in China

*Updated 28 September 2026. The revised Trademark Law takes effect **1 January 2027**. This guide explains which rules apply to a mark whose publication, opposition, invalidation or court case straddles that date.*

> **Status note.** The transition rules below come from two **draft Supreme People's Court (SPC) interpretations** published for public comment on **14 September 2026** — the *Provisions on the Temporal Application of the Trademark Law* and the revised *Provisions on Trademark Grant-and-Confirmation Administrative Cases*. Comments close **28 October 2026**. The final text may change; treat the specifics as draft until adopted. CNIPA is separately revising the implementing regulations and the Trademark Examination and Adjudication Guidelines and upgrading the online filing system.

## The one date that matters most: your preliminary-approval publication date

The single most common question during the switchover is whether an opponent has **3 months or 2 months** to file. Under the draft temporal-application rules (Article 7), the answer turns on the **date the mark is published for preliminary approval (初步审定公告日)**:

- **Preliminary-approval publication date before 1 January 2027** → the **three-month** opposition period under the 2019 Trademark Law applies.
- **Preliminary-approval publication date on or after 1 January 2027** → the new **two-month** opposition period applies.

In other words, a mark published in the last quarter of 2026 still carries the familiar three-month window even though the new Law is about to take effect. Do not assume the window has already shortened.

## How the draft rules assign the governing law

The draft temporal-application provisions use the time of the underlying legal act:

1. **General principle (Art. 1).** Civil disputes arising from legal facts that occur **after** the new Law takes effect are governed by the new Law; facts occurring **before** it are governed by the law then in force; facts that begin before and **continue** past the effective date are generally governed by the new Law.
2. **Refusal / non-registration review decisions (Art. 2).** Administrative suits over refusal-review or non-registration-review decisions apply the law in force **when the review decision was made**.
3. **Invalidation decisions (Art. 3).** Suits over invalidation-review decisions or invalidation rulings apply the law in force **when the registration-grant decision was made** (defined as the preliminary-approval publication, the refusal-review decision, or the registration/non-registration-review decision, depending on the route).
4. **Cancellation decisions (Art. 4).** Cancellation-review suits apply the law in force when the cancellation was requested or, for ex officio actions, when the process was initiated.
5. **Administrative penalties (Art. 5).** The law at the time of the infringing act applies, except where the law at the time of the penalty decision is lighter or no longer treats the act as unlawful.
6. **Procedural questions (Art. 6).** Procedural issues in the cases above are governed by the law in force when the administrative act was made.
7. **Invalidation time limit (Art. 7).** Whether an invalidation request is time-barred is judged under the law in force on the **registration publication date**. (A transitional clause covers marks first published within one year of the 2001 Law, applying that Law's five-year invalidation window.)
8. **Pending court cases (Art. 8).** First- and second-instance cases **not yet concluded** when the provisions take effect are governed by the new provisions. Two older SPC interpretations on jurisdiction and temporal application are repealed at the same time.

## Points from the revised grant-and-confirmation draft worth noting

- **Use on the register.** Use by the owner, by a licensee, or otherwise not against the owner's will counts as use; a mark used with minor differences that do not change its distinctive features is still treated as use of the registered mark. Mere assignment/licensing, or merely publishing registration information and asserting rights, does **not** count as use. This matters for both the existing 3-year non-use cancellation and the new ~5-year use declaration.
- **Copyright in a logo.** A copyright-registration certificate dated **before** the trademark application date can serve as preliminary evidence of ownership of the logo's copyright — a practical reason foreign brand owners register the logo's copyright in China.
- **Bad-faith / well-known marks.** The draft preserves the factors courts weigh for bad-faith squatting on well-known marks and for marks filed by an agent, representative, or party in a pre-contractual relationship without authorisation.

## Action checklist for foreign applicants and counsel

- If your mark (or a conflicting mark) receives **preliminary-approval publication in October–December 2026**, calendar the opposition deadline as **three months** from publication; verify the exact date on the China Trademark Network.
- For publications **from January 2027 onward**, use the **two-month** window — the deadline is shorter, so monitoring must be faster.
- For invalidation, identify the **registration publication date** to know which Law sets the five-year limit.
- If you have a case already in the administrative or court pipeline, expect matters **not concluded by 1 January 2027** to be assessed under the new provisions.
- Re-confirm everything after the SPC finalises the interpretations (the comment period ends 28 October 2026) and CNIPA issues the revised implementing regulations.
